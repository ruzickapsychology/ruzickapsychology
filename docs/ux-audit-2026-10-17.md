# UX and visual audit — October 17, 2026

## Plan and acceptance criteria

Audit the current production build locally with published CMS content. Preserve
the site's editorial/floral design and distinguish observed defects from taste
preferences. Do not publish unconfirmed practice facts or send real inquiries.

1. Inventory all public pages and capture mobile (375px), tablet (768px), and
   desktop (1440px) views. Stress the shared shell at 320px and short landscape.
2. Visually inspect page tops, content transitions, images, long text, and footers.
   Full-page captures establish coverage; viewport captures verify sticky elements
   and states that full-page images can hide.
3. Walk the service → pricing → contact journey, plus article discovery, FAQ
   expansion, mobile menu, keyboard focus, invalid form submission, and recovery.
4. Measure suspected clipping/overlap in the rendered DOM, reproduce each issue,
   and keep before/after evidence. Only fix evidence-backed problems.
5. Run automated responsive, accessibility, navigation, and mocked form checks.
   Add behavioral regressions for newly discovered failures where useful.
6. Rebuild and recheck affected pages, document outcomes and remaining limitations.

Pass criteria: readable and unobscured content; no horizontal page overflow;
coherent heading/spacing hierarchy; usable targets; visible keyboard focus;
predictable menu and scroll behavior; correct navigation destinations; form errors
retain user input and explain recovery; reduced-motion preferences respected.

Evidence is saved under `.local/ux-audit-2026-10-17/`. This review does not by itself
confirm deployment, real inbox delivery, or behavior on a physical iPhone.

## Results

Reviewed 15 published routes at 375, 768, and 1440 pixels (45 full-page
captures), plus 320px and 700×375 navigation/FAQ stress checks. Images were
scrolled into view before capture and checked for successful loading. No
horizontal page overflow was observed in the 45-view matrix.

Routes: home, About, Specialties, all four specialty detail pages, Pricing,
Contact, FAQ, Privacy, Blog, and the three currently published articles.

### Interaction and accessibility checks

The recorded journey follows home → Specialties → couples therapy → fees →
Contact. It expands service and insurance disclosures, checks required-field
validation, simulates a delivery failure, verifies retained input and focused
error feedback, and retries to a focused success confirmation. Every submission
is intercepted locally; no real inquiry is sent.

Automated checks also cover public-route accessibility, menu focus/inert behavior,
skip links, service-interest prefilling, headings, external destinations, image
loading, and responsive geometry. Safari-engine skip-link navigation uses
Option-Tab, matching WebKit's default link navigation, rather than assuming
Chrome's Tab behavior.

Generated Playwright reports and local audit artifacts are excluded from ESLint
so recording/trace output does not break the source-code lint check.

Full-page captures can place fixed headers at the captured scroll offset and
cannot establish sticky behavior by themselves. Viewport screenshots and scroll
measurements were used to verify the portrait and navigation findings. The Google
map loaded during direct inspection; its empty state in some early captures was
not treated as a defect.

No new practice-fact decisions were needed for these layout fixes. Existing
Christina questions remain in `docs/christina-review.md`. Real inbox delivery,
physical iPhone testing, and production deployment remain outside this local
verification. No claim is made that automated accessibility scans replace a
screen-reader user review.
